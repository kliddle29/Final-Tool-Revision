# Key Usability Findings

Full detail in `process/usability/usability-results.md`. Summary here.

## Tester

My dad, 57. Comfortable using a computer day to day with no hesitation
navigating one, though not deeply technical otherwise. Relies heavily
on reading glasses; spends hours a day on his computer for work, which
strains his eyes, and constantly has to grab his glasses or his phone
to use as a magnifying glass. The tool was built for him specifically.

## What went right

No hesitation at any point. He got the app working essentially
immediately, without help, from just a download link and no
explanation beforehand. Rated it 5/5 for how easy it was to figure out
without instructions, and 4/5 for how activating it made him feel. He
said he'd realistically use it for small text, unreadable fonts, or
enlarging a picture while browsing.

## The issue

Two of his six answers point at the same root cause. Asked what the
tool was trying to help him do, he said it was trying to "enlarge
certain objects on the screen, and to take photos of the screen as
well." Asked what felt confusing, he said "trying to figure out what
was in the selected region and what was not."

He wasn't describing the lens. He was describing macOS's own Screen
Recording capture, which the lens uses internally to work. The
system's real cursor was showing up doubled inside the zoomed view
(the actual cursor, plus a copy of it baked into the captured frame),
and there was no separate way to record a demo without running a
second screen-recording app alongside this one -- both read as the
tool being unclear about what it actually does.
